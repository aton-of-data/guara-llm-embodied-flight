1 · Open work items

Each item blocks a numbered risk or research question. Completion of any one of them changes a claim made elsewhere in this documentation set.

R-11blocks RQ4formal methods

Unblock CopilotVerifier

Monitors are generated from FRETish requirements through Ogma into Copilot C99 — and that C99 is currently trusted rather than proved, because the verifier toolchain has not been made to work here.

You need: Haskell, LLVM and z3, and the patience to make them agree with each other.
R-5airspace

Well-clear thresholds for small UAS

τ_daa currently rests on DO-365B thresholds written for considerably larger aircraft. Sourced, citable values appropriate to small multirotors would replace a parameter that is openly labelled a hypothesis.

You need: DAA or airspace-integration background, and access to the literature.
R-7simulation

Traffic injection in PX4 SITL

The DAA path has never faced a converging intruder in simulation, because there is no way to inject one. Building that path turns an untested branch into a measured one — and would also let R-6 (is Hold an adequate DAA recovery?) finally be answered with data.

You need: PX4 SITL internals, transponder message plumbing.
FM-12security

Authenticate the transport

An SROS2 profile for the complex-function and FMU topics. Until it exists, any node on the DDS domain can publish setpoints, and that is stated as an accepted limitation of this phase rather than omitted.

You need: SROS2, DDS security, ROS 2 Humble.
R-8standards

Review the F3269 mapping

The ASTM F3269 text is not available to this project. The whole role mapping is marked [REVIEW] and has never been checked against the licensed standard by someone holding it.

You need: Legitimate access to ASTM F3269, and an hour.
AC-48space

Orbital dynamics for the arbiter

A Basilisk ↔ ROS 2 setup that lets the existing decision core face real orbital dynamics instead of an analytic predictor. This is the bridge the whole space thread is waiting on.

You need: Basilisk, spacecraft dynamics, ROS 2 integration.
RS-1space

Review the safe-mode specification

The space recovery function does not exist in F´ and must be written. A safe mode with no flight heritage is the largest weakness in the orbital thread, and the project would much rather have it attacked now than discovered later.

You need: To have actually flown a spacecraft safe mode.
RQ6bLLM security

A jailbreak corpus for the mission compiler

The adversarial corpus exists but is small. The interesting question is not whether a model can be talked into something — RoboPAIR settled that — but whether anything it produces under attack survives the closed schema and the named checks.

You need: Red-teaming instincts and a text editor. This one is genuinely approachable.
G-K6specification

Write the switching core from the specification alone

A second implementation of the decision core, written from docs/SPEC.md and the header by a path independent of the existing code, to find out what the specification fails to say. Two ambiguities are already known and more are expected. The C ABI stays provisional until an independent port passes the conformance kit, so this work gates the interface everyone else would build against.

You need: A language of your choice and the discipline not to read core/. No hardware, no container, no ROS.
G-Z9space

A jailbreak corpus for the orbital vocabulary

The adversarial corpus is air-only: fields, spraying, ground sample distance, geofence. None of it can attack the space boundary. What is wanted is a slew whose endpoints are both legal and whose path crosses a keep-out, a request to release the safe-mode latch, a sequence whose members are individually valid and whose aggregate exhausts momentum.

You need: A text editor and an adversarial imagination. The most approachable item in the whole space thread.
G-Z13space

Measure whether safe-mode entry violates its own keep-out

A star tracker’s keep-out cone is declared around the body that blinds it, so a direct slew to a sun-pointing recovery attitude may sweep the very cone it must avoid. Sweep initial attitudes, count how many direct slews violate a cone, report the fraction and the worst margin. If it is above zero, the specified safe mode is unsafe for that fraction and the plan changes before any flight code is written.

You need: The analytic predictor that already ships, and a grid. No simulator, no F´ host.
G-K4G-H2hardware

First timing numbers that are not x86

Every latency figure this project publishes is software-in-the-loop on one x86 machine. Anyone with a small arm board can produce the first on-target measurements the kernel has ever had, through the conformance kit’s own benchmark. The result would be a measured worst observed time on real silicon — still not a bound, and a great deal better than nothing.

You need: A Raspberry Pi 5, a Jetson Orin Nano or similar, and a C++17 toolchain.
G-S8RH-1packaging

Port the packages ahead of the distribution’s end of life

ROS 2 Humble reaches end of life in May 2027, inside this programme’s horizon, while the pinned autopilot release recommends it. Making the packages build and pass the conformance kit on the next distribution retires the risk instead of accepting it.

You need: ROS 2 packaging experience and patience with build systems.
Where these come from

Each card names a row of the gap register, which lists every known absence in this project with the milestone that owns it. The whole register, grouped by track, is on the roadmap page.

Also always useful

A report from a setup that is not the author’s: a different airframe, a different distro, a different model behind the intent compiler. The evaluation harness ships a mock provider that needs no key and no network, so reproducing the pipeline costs you nothing but time.

2 · Mandatory review criteria

These five are conditions of acceptance rather than style preferences. Formatting, naming and structure are negotiable in review; these are not.

1

No number without a run

Performance figures come only from results/ via scripts/aggregate.py, and the reviewable part is published under docs/evidence/. Never write a measurement you did not produce. A contribution that quietly widens a claim is the one kind of change that damages this project.

2

No API from memory

Every behavioural claim about PX4, ROS 2, F´, Ogma, Copilot, DAIDALUS or FRET cites repo@commit:file:line in GROUNDING.md, against shallow clones pinned in third_party/VERSIONS.md. If your change touches an API, add the GROUNDING.md row first.

3

Every safety function has a test that fails before the implementation

Write the failing test, watch it fail, then implement. For a compiler check specifically, the test must construct a case where your check is the only failing one.

4

The decision path allocates nothing and has bounded time

DecisionCore and everything it calls per tick. test_decision_core_no_alloc.cpp and test_decision_core_timing.cpp enforce it, so this is checked rather than trusted.

5

A new flight operation gets its document before it gets code

One document per operation, air and space, under docs/operations/, with its checks, channels, recovery function and evidence. The template is here.

Mechanics: C++17 with colcon/ament, gtest and launch_testing. Granular commits, one logical change each, messages as type(scope): imperative summary. Every new source file starts with SPDX-License-Identifier: Apache-2.0. Nothing under the NASA Open Source Agreement may live outside nosa/, and no GPL-2.0-only dependency may enter the tree at all — both are enforced by a script, not by convention. By contributing you agree your contribution is licensed under Apache-2.0.

3 · Challenge a claim

There is a dedicated issue template for this, and it is the contribution the project values most highly: “claim challenge”. If a number on this site, in the README or in the SPEC does not follow from the evidence directory it cites, that is a defect of the same severity as a crash.

Claim challenge

A stated claim is not supported by its evidence, or the evidence does not say what the text says it says.

Bug report

Something is wrong in the code. Include the seed and the run directory — a defect without a reproducible run is much weaker.

Operation or capability

You want a new flight operation or capability pack. This template asks for the document’s content before any code.

Security

Do not open a public issue for a vulnerability. SECURITY.md has the private disclosure address, the accepted limitations of this phase — FM-12, unauthenticated DDS, above all — and what does count as a physical-authority escape.

4 · Getting set up

The trusted mission layer is pure Python and needs no container. This is enough to work on the compiler, the corpus, the plan executor and the space keep-out predictor:

no container required
pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
python3 -m pytest scripts/tests -q
python3 -m mission.compiler --intent mission/intents/survey_north_3.json

Everything else runs in Docker with the repository mounted at /work:

the full bench
# amd64 only, published by hand, and possibly behind the tree (gap G-S4).
# If the pull fails or the image is stale, build locally instead: ./scripts/dev.sh --build
docker pull ghcr.io/aton-of-data/guara-dev:m1
export GUARA_IMAGE=ghcr.io/aton-of-data/guara-dev:m1

./scripts/fetch_third_party.sh
./scripts/dev.sh colcon build --symlink-install
./scripts/dev.sh colcon test --packages-select guara_rta guara_geofence guara_monitors
./scripts/sitl_run.sh --scenario hover --seed 42 --headless

The formal-methods image is not published: it embeds FRET, which is under the NASA Open Source Agreement, and distributing a container that embeds NOSA code needs a legal review that is tracked as an open risk. Build it locally with ./scripts/fm.sh.

5 · Funding

Guará is Apache-2.0 and independent. Most of it is work that only costs time — but the parts that stand between a research prototype and something an operator could trust are physical, and they cost money.

Hardware

A hardware-in-the-loop bench

Every latency number on this site is software-in-the-loop on one machine. Re-measuring δ_lat on a real companion computer, over a real link, against a real FMU is the single change that would most improve what this project is allowed to claim.

Hardware

A reference airframe and a flight campaign

SITL does not transfer, and this is stated on every page. A first real flight campaign — with an airframe, a site, insurance and a licensed pilot — is what turns “measured in simulation” into evidence.

Access

Licensed standards

The ASTM F3269 text is not available to this project, which is why its own central role mapping carries a [REVIEW] marker. Standards access is unglamorous and it unblocks a risk immediately.

People

Review time from people who have certified avionics

The failure modes, the safe-mode specification and the switching properties all benefit far more from an experienced reader than from more code. Funded review hours buy exactly that.

Terms

No component is sold, gated, or withheld from the public tree for funders; the licence is Apache-2.0 and remains so. Funding affects rate rather than access: measurements are made sooner, on better hardware, and reviewed by more people.

6 · Scope and conduct

Exclusions — not subject to negotiation

Civil flight safety only. Contributions implementing weapons or any payload intended to harm, target selection, tracking or identification of specific people for enforcement or harassment, covert surveillance, or the defeat of geofences, remote ID or failsafes will not be accepted.

The CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md applies to every space this project occupies. In review, the project’s own bias runs toward the person who says a number is wrong — see the review that invalidated twelve of its own rows.

7 · Citing

There is no preprint yet. Cite the repository and the commit, via CITATION.cff. Every quantitative claim should cite the run directory under docs/evidence/ that produced it — not this site, and not the README.
